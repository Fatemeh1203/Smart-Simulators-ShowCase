01 / Sensor Errors

Sensor Errors: Why Raw Numbers Lie

Bias · Gain Error · Drift
WHAT IS IT?
A sensor never gives the true value directly. Raw output = true value + random noise + systematic errors. You can remove noise by averaging, but systematic errors stay in place and can only be tracked through state estimation.
Like a kitchen scale that always reads 50g light — even if you weigh a hundred times, those 50g stay put. That's bias.

02 / Kalman Filter

Kalman Filter: Two Sources, One Better Estimate

Predict · Update · Kalman Gain
WHAT IS IT?
You have two sources: a physics model says 'five minutes ago it was 22°, so probably 22.5° now', and a thermometer says 23° (but noisy). Kalman filter takes a weighted average — the weight depends on how much you trust each one.
Like two friends: one says 10:00, the other 10:15 — if you trust them equally, you say 10:07; if the first is always 5 min fast, you weight the second more. K is that weight.
x̂ = x̂predict + K × (z − x̂predict)

03 / Neutron Flux

Neutron Flux: The Reactor's Heartbeat

Neutron Flux · Reactor Power · Incore/Excore
WHAT IS IT?
Neutron flux = number of neutrons crossing a unit area per unit time. It's directly tied to reactor power: higher flux means a stronger chain reaction. Incore sensors sit inside the core, Excore sensors sit outside — both report to the control room.
Like a car's speedometer: the number on the dashboard results from thousands of events per second. A faulty speedo means wrong decisions — in a reactor, that threatens safety.

04 / Fiber Optic Sensor

Fiber Optic Sensor: Reading Phenomena Through Light Spectra

Modal Interference · FBG · Spectrum
WHAT IS IT?
Multiple optical modes travel through a fiber at slightly different speeds. When temperature or strain affects the fiber, the relative phase of these modes shifts. At the fiber end, their interference creates a spectral pattern that carries the signature of all disturbances combined.
Like throwing stones into a pond — each wave from one point interferes with others, and the final pattern is the sum of all those stones.

05 / Digital Twin

Digital Twin: In Step With Reality

Streaming · Pipeline · Residual Monitoring
WHAT IS IT?
Digital twin = a fit-for-purpose digital representation of a physical entity, synchronized in real-time. The key difference from batch: raw data is processed at the moment of production, not accumulated then processed later.
Like a mirror showing your real reflection — not yesterday's photo. If the mirror takes an hour to update, it's no longer a mirror, it's a historical report.

06 / IEC 61850

Three-Tier Smart Substation Architecture

Process · Bay · Station
WHAT IS IT?
IEC 61850 is the communication standard for digital substations. The architecture is split into three tiers: Process (sensors), Bay (intelligent devices), and Station (SCADA). Your fiber optic sensor naturally sits at the Process level.

Architecture Tiers — Click Each

Level Details: Station Level

  • Protocol:MMS (Manufacturing Message Specification)
  • Equipment:SCADA, HMI, central database
  • Latency:Hundreds of ms to seconds
  • Role:Control room & reporting
  • For Your Chapter:Natural home for Digital Twin & UKF
These protocols' security is covered by IEC 62351 — the smart-grid counterpart of IEC 63096 in the nuclear world.
Sources: IEC 61850 · IEC 61850-8-1 (GOOSE & MMS) · IEC 61850-9-2 (SV) · IEC 62351 (Security)
